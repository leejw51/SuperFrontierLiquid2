Lacquer & Neon

SuperFrontierLiquid is a professional instrument that strangers walk in off the street to use. The instrument wins on the terminal screen; the stranger wins everywhere else. Landing does all the teaching, so the terminal never has to apologise for being dense. Comfortable density is the default because a beginner arrives before a professional does, and compact is one switch away and remembered.

Materials, not moods

Black lacquer for the surfaces, bone for the type, jade for the bid, vermilion for the ask, brass for anything that wants your attention. Five materials with a shop-front logic to them: the palette is named after things, so a new colour has to justify itself as a new material.

Market colour never touches chrome

Jade and vermilion only ever mean direction. Buttons, links, focus rings, selected tabs and the wordmark are bone and graphite. A green button therefore always means buy, and nothing else in the product is ever green.

Radius encodes interactivity

Panels, tables and the chart are square. Anything you can click is 4px. Anything that floats above the page is 6px. Anything round is a state, not a container. You can tell what is clickable with the colour switched off.

The chain has a rail, not a page

Block height, throughput and the last twelve blocks live on a 40px rail across the bottom of every screen. The blockchain underneath is a continuous fact, not a tab you have to go and find.

What we deliberately did not do

RejectedBecause
Blue-black canvasIt is the default surface of every exchange front end. Warm graphite at hue 25° gives jade and vermilion somewhere of their own to sit, and reads as lacquer rather than as a screen.
A saturated brand accentIn a trading UI the loudest colours must belong to the market. The brand speaks through form: the spine, the tape rule, the square panel, the rail.
A fifth hue for liquidationA second red is indistinguishable from the first under protanopia (ΔE 5.0). Liquidation danger is vermilion plus a 135° hazard texture plus the word. Texture survives every form of colour blindness.
Cards with shadowsA terminal is a window manager, not a feed. Panels are flush, square, and separated by a one-pixel seam of canvas. Shadow is reserved for things that genuinely float.

Colour

Every text token below was measured against the canvas, the panel, the raised surface and the hover surface in both themes; the lowest ratio in that set is the one quoted. All pass WCAG AA for normal text at 4.5:1, which is the bar even at 11px. Flip the theme in the header to check any of it.

The buy and sell pair separates by ΔE 32.2 for normal vision, 12.7 under deuteranopia and 11.9 under protanopia, measured in OKLab. That clears the threshold on its own — but colour still never carries meaning alone.

Price convention

Jade rises and vermilion falls, which is the convention most of the world's markets read. Markets across Greater China read the opposite way. The convention is a two-variable swap, not a redesign: setting --bid to the vermilion ramp and --ask to the jade ramp in a [data-convention="cjk"] block reverses the whole product, because nothing else in the system is allowed to hard-code either value.

Type

Two roles, no downloads. The interface face is the platform grotesque, which is the fastest-rendering and most familiar shape available and needs no network request on a screen where the first paint is a price. The numeric face is the platform monospace, and it is reserved — digits, hashes, symbols and identifiers only, never prose and never labels. That is what keeps a price from shifting sideways when 63,999 becomes 64,000.

Numerals

Everything that can change wears --font-num with font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums and slashed zero. Prices are right-aligned in every table so the decimal point forms a column. Trailing zeros are never trimmed — the API sends "64250.50" and the UI shows 64,250.50, because a price that drops a digit reads as a different price at a glance.

Capitals

Letterspaced capitals are banned as a decorative device. They appear in exactly one place: instrument tags of four characters or fewer — perp spot gtc ioc — where they are the market's own notation rather than a label we invented. Everything else is sentence case, including buttons and column headers.

Density

Two modes, one switch, remembered per user. Comfortable ships on by default: a new player has never seen an order book and needs room to read one. Compact takes a 1920×1080 terminal from about 21 visible order rows to 32, and is where a regular will live. Every measurement in both modes is a token, so a component never needs to know which mode it is in. Toggle it in the header of this page and watch every screen below re-lay itself.

Row heights are fixed, never content-driven. That is not an aesthetic preference: the orders, fills and ledger tables are virtualised, and virtualisation needs top = index × var(--row-h) to be true. A row that grows to fit its content breaks the scroller. Anything that will not fit is truncated with an ellipsis and available in the row's tooltip.

Button

One primary action per region. Labels name what will happen, in sentence case, in the same words the confirmation will use — Place order produces Order placed. No arrows appended to labels.

Virtualisation contract: the scroll body is height: rows × var(--row-h), each visible row is absolutely positioned at top: index × var(--row-h), and a nine-row overscan sits either side of the viewport. Loading, empty and error

Skeletons take the exact shape of the content they replace, at the current density's row height, so nothing jumps when the data lands. Empty states are an invitation to act. Error states say what happened and what to do, in the interface's voice, and never apologise.

The nine states a trading interface lives or dies by

Each one names the field it is derived from, so there is no state in the design that the frozen API cannot actually produce. Nothing here is inferred from a field that does not exist.

StateDerived fromTreatment
Socket disconnectedWebSocket close, or no pong within 90sTop-bar indicator turns vermilion and reads Offline with the time it dropped. All prices dim to --text-muted and stop flashing. The order ticket's submit button disables with the reason inline. Nothing is hidden — the last known book stays on screen, visibly frozen.
ReconnectingBackoff attempt counterIndicator turns brass, heartbeat speeds to 700ms, label reads Reconnecting · attempt 3. On success the gateway sends a fresh snapshot on every channel, so the book is replaced wholesale rather than patched, and the panel briefly shows the Resynced badge.
Stale priceTime since the last ticker message > 5s, or a 422 ORACLE_STALE on submitThe price keeps its last value — never blanked — and gains a brass rule beneath it plus an age counter. Order entry stays enabled, because the node may still accept, but the ticket shows the brass banner below.
Pending on chainstatus: "pending", before accepted_atThe row appears immediately at 60% opacity with a signal-blue Pending badge and the truncated tx_hash. Cancel is disabled until acceptance, because there is nothing yet to cancel.
Acceptedstatus: "open" with tx_hash and accepted_atOpacity returns to full, the badge becomes Open, and the row flashes its side tint once. The block height from the fill is shown in the toast as the receipt.
Partial fillfilled_qty vs qtyThe filled fraction is drawn as a jade or vermilion fill across the left of the size cell, with the percentage in text beside it. A partially filled order is never rendered in the same style as an unfilled one.
RejectedThe non-2xx error.codeA toast whose title is the plain-language consequence and whose body is the gateway's own message plus, wherever the code allows one, a single-tap remedy: reprice for POST_ONLY_WOULD_CROSS, open the faucet for INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE, reduce size for INSUFFICIENT_MARGIN.
Approaching liquidationliquidation_risk, with margin_ratio and mark_price against liquidation_priceFour steps, below. The escalation is texture and placement, not a redder red.
Settlingstatus: "settling"The window between expiry and the settlement transaction landing. The book is dimmed and un-clickable, the market carries a signal-blue Settling badge, and open positions read Settlement pending in place of an unrealised figure — because there is no longer a mark price to compute one from.
Halted, expired, settledmarkets[].statusThe book is dimmed and un-clickable, the ticket is replaced by an explanation, and the market keeps its place in the list marked Halted or Settled. Positions in a settled market show the settlement price and the realised amount, and the row is no longer live.

Why the words sit the way they do

Two failure modes at once. Lose the phrase and the account is gone; let someone read it and the account is theirs. Every decision below is aimed at one or the other, and a wall of red warnings is aimed at neither — people learn to click past those in a week.

DecisionReasoning
Three across, four down, read like a line of textThe dominant transcription error on paper is order, not spelling: BIP-39 words are unique in their first four letters, so a misspelling is caught by the wordlist on the way back in, while a swapped pair is caught by nothing until the checksum fails months later. A column-major grid invites the eye to read across it and pick up 01 then 07. Row-major, three wide, is the only arrangement whose natural reading order and whose numbering agree, and three columns is short enough that the eye finds the start of the next row without a ruler.
The ordinal is the primary keyZero-padded, tabular, in --font-num, in its own 20px gutter to the left of the word. You transcribe 07 marble, not marble. Zero padding makes 01 and 12 the same width so the numbers form a true column, and the rule under each row of three keeps the return sweep honest — the ledger ruling is doing the same job here that it does in a table.
The first four letters carry full contrastEvery word in the BIP-39 English list is unique in its first four letters. Setting those four in --text-primary and the tail in --text-secondary tells the reader exactly which part has to be right, and quietly reassures the person who is not sure whether they wrote quantum or quantam. It is the only typographic emphasis on the screen.
Monospace, and the same monospace as a priceThe rule in this system is that --font-num is for digits, hashes, symbols and identifiers. A mnemonic is an identifier. Fixed advance width also means every word occupies a measurable amount of space, so a missing letter shows up as a short word rather than as nothing.
The phrase is covered until it is asked for, and covers itself againIt is not on screen while the page loads, while a screenshot is taken of something else, or while the operator walks away. A 90-second timer and a blur of the window both re-cover it, and the timer is visible so the re-cover is never a surprise. This is the concrete answer to do not let the phrase sit on screen longer than it needs to.
Copy exists; download does notA password manager is the most reliable place a phrase can live, and a product that refuses to offer Copy pushes people to take a screenshot instead — which lands in a cloud photo library, a sync backup and an OCR index in one move. So the button exists, it is secondary, it names its destination (Copy for a password manager), and the line under it says plainly that the clipboard is readable by other applications. Download as a file is removed: a seed phrase sitting in a downloads folder is the worst outcome of the three.
The danger is one paragraph, not a banner stackOne brass icon in the modal head, one sentence that names both failures in the user's own terms — anyone who reads them can trade as you, and if you lose them nobody can give the account back — and then nothing else shouts. Brass is the system's attention material and vermilion is not available: vermilion means direction, and the hazard texture is reserved for money about to be lost.
The reveal screen carries no other fieldNo handle, no checkbox, no marketing. The handle is asked on the next step. A screen that asks for two things gets half the attention on each.

Why three words instead of a checkbox

The old design ended with I have saved my recovery code somewhere safe and a disabled button. A checkbox proves intent and nothing else, and it is exactly the control people learn to click past. Re-entering three words proves possession, takes about twelve seconds, and catches the specific failure a checkbox cannot: three ordinals spread across three different rows and three different columns means a phrase transcribed in the wrong order fails here, in the one minute when the words are still on the desk, rather than on the day the account is needed back.

That is word 08 — check for a skipped word

The error names the word the person actually typed rather than saying incorrect, because that is word 08 locates the mistake instead of just reporting one: they are a line out, so the whole tail of what they wrote is probably shifted by one. A bare incorrect would send them back to a phrase they can no longer see with nothing to look for. After three attempts the words are shown again rather than the account being abandoned.

The paste path, for someone with a password manager

DecisionReasoning
One field, not twelveTwelve boxes look careful and are hostile to the only tool that reliably stores a phrase. A password manager holds one string; the field takes one string. It normalises on paste: numbered-list prefixes stripped, line breaks and runs of whitespace collapsed to single spaces, case folded down. Someone pasting 1. ripple
2. cactus from a note gets a valid phrase, not an error.
Masked by default, with a Show toggleThe same reason a password field is masked: the common case for pasting is a laptop in a room with other people. Word count and validity are computed while masked, so the field tells you it is right without showing you what it is.
Three checks, in escalating orderCount as you type — 9 of 12 words. Wordlist per word, naming the offender and its nearest neighbour: word 4, “recieve”, is not a BIP-39 word — did you mean receive? Checksum when twelve valid words are present, which is the only check that catches an ordering mistake: all twelve words are real, but the phrase does not check out — two of them are in the wrong order, or one is the wrong word.
The derived address is shown before anything is signedThis is the transcription check for the paste path, and it is the strongest one available: the address is a function of the whole phrase, so a person who recognises their own address has verified all twelve words at once. It is drawn in signal blue because nothing has been settled with the server yet.
An account index control, defaulted to zeroThe single most common support question for an EVM login is why is this not my address, and the answer is almost always that the wallet is on another index of the same phrase. The control is small, sits beside the address it changes, and the hint says to leave it alone.

The signature step

The gateway's message is shown verbatim, in the sunken surface used for hashes, because the contract requires the client to sign the string it was given rather than rebuild it — and a person is entitled to read what they are signing. The copy beside it says what the signature does not do: it costs nothing, moves nothing, and authorises nothing but this login. There is no wallet prompt to explain away, because the key is already in the tab.

A new user reaching this screen through the paste path is the one case where the client cannot know in advance that an account is being created, so the handle field appears only after the gateway answers 422 HANDLE_REQUIRED, with a brass banner explaining why it has suddenly appeared. That is the whole of what registration is in this product: there is no separate registration step, and the design must not imply one.

The phrase on a phone

Transcription to paper happens more often on a phone than on a desktop, so the reveal has to survive 390. The grid drops to two columns, which keeps the ordinal and the word on one line at 15px and keeps the row rule readable; the reading order and the numbering still agree.

What the landing page claims, and what it no longer claims

DecisionReasoning
The hero is the productA trading product explained in stock photography is a trading product nobody believes. The book on the right is the production component at production density; it is the strongest honest claim the page can make.
Two paths, neither of them a formThere is no handle field on the landing page any more. A handle is still required on a first login, but it is asked once the client knows an account is being created — on step three of the new-wallet flow, or after a 422 on the paste path. Putting it in the hero would ask every returning user for something they already have.
The third figure changedIt used to read 0 — cost, no wallet, no key management. That is now false: there is a wallet, and the key is the user's problem in exactly the way it is in every other EVM product. It reads 0 personal data instead, which is still true and is the better claim anyway.
The identity explanation is made earlyThe hint under the paths names BIP-39, the Ethereum derivation path, and the use of disposable test wallets. It states that this is a local experiment and that real assets and their recovery phrases must stay out of it.
The three explainers are componentsA faucet grant row, a position row and the chain rail — lifted directly out of the app. The stranger has seen three real screens before signing up.
No countdown, no scarcity, no waitlistIt is an open-source simulator. The honest pitch is that it is free and nothing is at stake, and that is more persuasive than urgency.

The two addresses, and the sentence that has to land first

A person who opens this page and sees an unfamiliar address holding their balance will conclude they have been robbed, and they will conclude it in about a second. The panel is built so that the reassurance arrives in the same glance as the problem.

DecisionReasoning
Two cards side by side, not a list of fieldsThe question is not what are my addresses, it is why are there two. Two objects with an arrow between them is a picture of a relationship; two rows of a definition list is not. The arrow is labelled computed from the one on the left, so the direction of the relationship is stated rather than implied.
Each card leads with who holds the keyYou hold the key and the gateway holds the key are set at body size above the address itself, because that is the actual difference between them and the hex string is not. The spines carry it too: bone for the one that is yours to sign with, brass for the one the system operates.
The reassurance is the first clause of the noteYour balance sits at the trading address, and nothing has been moved. The explanation of the derivation comes second. A person who is frightened reads the first clause and stops; a person who is curious keeps going.
Determinism is stated in the user's own termsNot HKDF-SHA256 of the server secret and your login address but one login address always maps to this same trading account, and signing in with the same twelve words on another machine brings you back here. The mechanism is in the contract; the guarantee is what belongs on the page.
The trade-off is admitted, with a pointerThe note says plainly that a production exchange would take orders signed by the user's own key, and names the section of the contract where that is written up. A simulator that hides its custodial shortcut is a simulator nobody should trust about anything else.
Neither address is truncated hereElsewhere in the product an address is 0x8F3B…c21D. On this page both are shown in full, because this is the one screen whose job is to let a person compare an address against what their wallet shows.

Block height and transaction hash close every row, because every movement in this product is traceable to a block.

Why this screen is not the terminal

DecisionReasoning
Six tiles, one number eachThe overview answers one question — is anything wrong — and it has to answer it from across a room. Each tile leads with a single figure at --fs-xl and demotes its supporting numbers to --fs-xs rows. The tiles are one gutter apart on a canvas seam, in the same flush arrangement panels use everywhere else.
One tile is allowed to shoutaccounts_below_maintenance is the only figure on the overview that is a decision rather than a fact, so it takes a --brass-wash fill and a route into the user list. It is a route and not a filter: /admin/users takes query and is_bot and nothing else, so the button reads Open the user list rather than promising a filter the gateway cannot serve. The others stay flat however large they get, because a number that is large is not the same as a number that is wrong.
Component health is a shape and a wordA row of green ticks would break the first rule in this system: green means buy and nothing else. So ok is a muted tick, degraded is a brass triangle and down is a vermilion cross, each beside the word, with the gateway's detail string in monospace after it. Health reads identically in greyscale. The brass is on the glyph and never on the word, because §3 forbids brass as text in the light theme — Degraded is set in ink and takes its emphasis from weight. The same rule governs the settle dialog's deviation figure and the stale oracle age in the markets table, which is a badge warn rather than a brass number.
The degraded component gets a banner, not just a rowA status list is where you go to check; a banner is what finds you. degraded or down raises a brass banner above the overview naming the component, the age and what the operator can do about it, with the actions attached. Everything ok and the banner is not drawn at all.
Destructive controls stay visible and disabledSettle is present and disabled wherever it does not apply, with the reason on the control: on a market that is still trading, and on every perpetual, because the endpoint force-settles a dated future and a perpetual has no expiry to settle at. A control that disappears sends the operator hunting, and hunting under pressure is how the wrong button gets pressed. This is the same rule the order ticket follows: a disabled button never hides its reason.
Confirmations ignore the density switchCompact exists to fit more rows on a screen. A confirmation wants fewer things on a screen. Every destructive dialog here carries data-density-lock="comfortable", which re-declares the comfortable density values locally. It is the only scope in the system permitted to override the user's density preference, and it may not be used on a table.
The audit actor is an addressA handle can be changed with one PATCH; an address cannot change at all. Who halted this market has to still have an answer after somebody renames themselves.
The address column is the login address/admin/users[].address is the address the person signed in with — the same field /user/profile returns. Labelling it Login address rather than Address keeps the distinction the account page spends a whole panel establishing.
Bots are badged here tooSame badge, same signal blue, same strategy name as the leaderboard. An operator filtering for abusive order flow needs to see at a glance that the account submitting eight thousand orders an hour is one of ours.

Force-settle — catching a mistyped price before it is applied

Force-settling a dated future closes every position at a price the operator types, and it cannot be undone. The price is the whole risk: every other field in the dialog is already decided. Five devices stack against a typo, and none of them is a wall of text.

DeviceWhat it catches
The price in wordsthree thousand two hundred against thirty-two thousand. A dropped or doubled digit is invisible in a column of tabular figures and unmissable in English. This is the single most effective control in the dialog and it costs one line.
Live deviation from the oracle markThe percentage is computed as you type against last oracle mark, which the market row already carries. Inside 2% it is muted, beyond 2% brass, beyond 10% vermilion. It names both numbers so the comparison is on screen rather than in the operator's head.
The gateway's own 10% bandThe contract rejects a price more than 10% from the last mark unless force: true is sent. The dialog renders that as a second, explicit act — a checkbox whose label repeats both prices — instead of quietly attaching the flag. An override that takes no extra effort is not an override.
Double entryThe price is typed twice, into fields that never show each other. This is the oldest control in accounting and it is here for the same reason: a slip of the hand does not reproduce itself. A mismatch blocks the button and says which field is empty or different.
The button carries the numberSettle ETH-261225 at 3,200.00. The last thing under the cursor repeats the market and the price, so the final glance before the click is at the value rather than at the word Confirm.
Consequences before controlsThe dialog opens with what will happen — every position closed, the difference paid out, zero open interest, no undo — and only then shows open interest, resting orders and the mark. /admin/markets does not return a position count, so the dialog does not claim one; it shows open interest, which it does return.

Raising a maintenance requirement can put open accounts below maintenance immediately, and PATCH /admin/markets/{symbol}/risk returns accounts_now_below_maintenance so the operator finds out before the liquidation engine does. The task is to make that number land where the decision is being made.

DecisionReasoning
Every parameter shows now and new on one line25× → 20×. A form that shows only the new value asks the operator to remember the old one, and the old one is the whole basis of the judgement.
The count that exists before is shown before/admin/overview already carries accounts_below_maintenance for the whole system. It is not a preview of this change, and the banner does not pretend it is — it is the baseline the new figure will be read against, and having it on screen means the after number is a delta rather than a bare shock.
The dialog does not close on saveIt becomes its own result. The consequence is the reason the endpoint returns a number at all, so putting it in a toast — a thing that dwells five seconds and leaves — would be throwing away the only warning the contract gives.
The count is the headline of the resultThe figure is rendered at display size in vermilion beside what it was before, so the change reads as a delta rather than as a bare shock. It is not accompanied by a list of accounts: the response does not contain one and /admin/users carries no margin ratio to derive one from. A brass note says exactly that, so nobody reads the missing list as an omission.
There is a way back, and it is honest about its limitsRestore 25× · 400 · 200 is a second PATCH with the values the dialog still holds. The copy says plainly that restoring does not un-liquidate anything that has already happened, because between the two requests is at least one block.
What this design cannot do, and whyThe contract has no dry run: accounts_now_below_maintenance arrives with applied: true, never before it, and it is a count rather than a list. Inventing either in the design would put a promise on screen that the gateway cannot keep, so per §12 they are raised here rather than designed around, in order of value: (1) a ?dry_run=true on the risk PATCH, which turns an after-the-fact report into a preview and is the smallest change that would make this flow genuinely safe; (2) the affected user_ids in the response, because 3 accounts is a statistic and three handles at 84.2%, 81.9% and 80.6% is a decision, and the operator cannot get from one to the other with the endpoints that exist.
